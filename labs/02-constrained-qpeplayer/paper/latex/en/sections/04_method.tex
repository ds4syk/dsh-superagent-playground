\section{Method and Theory}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Outer loop: Rockafellar--Wets ALM}\label{sec:alm}
With $\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathbb{R}^p_{\ge0}$ and $\rho>0$, the ALM uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_\rho(\mathbf q,\mathbf t,\boldsymbol\mu)=J_\theta(\mathbf q,\mathbf t)+\frac{1}{2\rho}\sum_{j=1}^p\Big(\big[\mu_j+\rho g_j(\mathbf q,\mathbf t)\big]_+^2-\mu_j^2\Big).
\label{eq:alm}
\end{equation}
Each iteration identifies $\mathcal A=\{j:\mu_j+\rho g_j>0\}$, assembles the coefficients below, solves the inner problem with $\mathcal A$ frozen, then sets $\mu_j\leftarrow[\mu_j+\rho g_j]_+$ and raises $\rho$ if the violation does not fall. Freezing $\mathcal A$ is an active-set device: the frozen minimiser is a local minimiser of \eqref{eq:alm} iff $\operatorname{sign}(\mu_j+\rho g_j)$ matches $\mathcal A$ for all $j$; otherwise we iterate. In practice 2--5 inner iterations per outer step suffice.

\subsection{Theorem 2.1: the inner problem has $p$-independent dimension}\label{sec:dim}
\begin{theorem}[Constant-dimension reduction]\label{thm:dim}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:main} hold, fix $\boldsymbol\mu,\rho$ and an active set $\mathcal A$. On the region where the active pattern is $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal L_\rho|_{\mathcal A}=\mathbf v_2^\top\mathbf M_{\mathcal A}\mathbf v_2+2\mathbf t^\top\mathbf D_{\mathcal A}\mathbf v_2+\mathbf t^\top\mathbf G_{\mathcal A}\mathbf t+\kappa_{\mathcal A}$, with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf M_{\mathcal A}&=\mathbf M_0+\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}\Big[\tfrac{\mu_j}{2}\big(\mathbf e_{\rm tr}\tilde{\mathbf a}_j^\top+\tilde{\mathbf a}_j\mathbf e_{\rm tr}^\top\big)+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\tilde{\mathbf a}_j\tilde{\mathbf a}_j^\top\Big],\\
\mathbf D_{\mathcal A}&=\mathbf D_0+\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}\Big[\tfrac{\mu_j}{2}\mathbf b_j\mathbf e_{\rm tr}^\top+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\mathbf b_j\tilde{\mathbf a}_j^\top\Big],\qquad
\mathbf G_{\mathcal A}=\mathbf G_0+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}\mathbf b_j\mathbf b_j^\top,\\
\kappa_{\mathcal A}&=-\frac{1}{2\rho}\sum_{j\notin\mathcal A}\mu_j^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:MA}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf G_{\mathcal A}\succ0$ then $\min_{\mathbf t}\mathcal L_\rho|_{\mathcal A}=\mathbf v_2^\top\mathbf M^{\rm red}_{\mathcal A}\mathbf v_2+\kappa_{\mathcal A}$ with $\mathbf M^{\rm red}_{\mathcal A}=\mathbf M_{\mathcal A}-\mathbf D_{\mathcal A}^\top\mathbf G_{\mathcal A}^{-1}\mathbf D_{\mathcal A}$ and $\mathbf t^\star=-\mathbf G_{\mathcal A}^{-1}\mathbf D_{\mathcal A}\mathbf v_2(\mathbf q)$, so the inner problem is a \emph{quartic form on $\mathbb{S}^3$} of dimension independent of $p$.
\end{theorem}
The expansion uses the hinge identity, $\mathbf e_{\rm tr}^\top\mathbf v_2=1$ and the $\mathbf t$-stationarity. Three cautions. \textbf{(a)} $\kappa_{\mathcal A}$ must sum over \emph{inactive} $j$ only; the symmetric choice changes $\mathcal L_\rho|_{\mathcal A}$ by $\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}\mu_j^2/(2\rho)$ and perturbs the multiplier recursion (error $5.57\times10^{-1}$ versus $3.55\times10^{-15}$). \textbf{(b)} The form is quadratic in $\mathbf v_2$ but \emph{quartic in $\mathbf q$}---the form QPEP reaches after eliminating $\mathbf t$. The KKT system is four cubics plus one quadratic (Bezout bound $3^4\cdot2=162$), whereas QPEP returns at most 40 roots; Thm.~\ref{thm:spectrum} shows the generic count stays 40, and a safe implementation regenerates the template for the actual monomial support, still $O(1)$ in $p$. \textbf{(c)} Freezing $\mathcal A$ selects one piece of the piecewise-quadratic \eqref{eq:alm}; the consistency check above decides whether its minimiser is a local minimiser.

\subsection{Theorem 2.2: what ``globally optimal'' can and cannot mean}\label{sec:conditional}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional global optimality]\label{thm:cond}
Let $\hat{\mathbf z}$ be the ALM output with $\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}\ge0$ and violation $\nu=\max_j[g_j(\hat{\mathbf z})]_+$, and for $\boldsymbol\mu\ge0$ let $d(\boldsymbol\mu)=\min_{\mathbb{S}^3\times\mathbb{R}^3}\{J_\theta+\sum_j\mu_jg_j\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
d(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})\ \le\ J^\star\ \le\ J_\theta(\hat{\mathbf z})+L\nu ,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
0\le J_\theta(\hat{\mathbf z})-J^\star\le \underbrace{J_\theta(\hat{\mathbf z})-d(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})}_{=:\delta}+L\nu .
\label{eq:sandwich}
\end{equation}
If $\delta+L\nu\le\tau$ then $\hat{\mathbf z}$ is $\tau$-globally optimal; in exact arithmetic $\delta\ge0$, and a computed $\delta<0$ is a numerical artefact.
\end{theorem}
This is the content of ALM plus a dual bound, but it is not a certificate: $d(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})$ is itself a global solve of a non-convex problem, implemented by QPEP-style enumeration with no certificate. On our synthetic instance $\delta=-1.67\times10^{-11}$---negative, hence unusable, and evidence that the numerical error in $d$ exceeds the gap.

\subsection{Lemma 2.2: a sound certificate with a $p$-independent PSD block}\label{sec:cert}
\begin{lemma}[Sound $14\times14$ certificate]\label{lem:cert}
Let $\mathbf x=(\mathbf v_2(\mathbf q),\mathbf t)\in\mathbb{R}^{13}$, $\hat{\mathbf x}=(\mathbf x,1)$, $\mathbf X=\hat{\mathbf x}\hat{\mathbf x}^\top$, and let $\hat{\mathbf Q}_\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{14}$ be the homogenised matrix of $J_\theta$. Consider
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Shor}=\min_{\mathbf X}\ &\langle\hat{\mathbf Q}_\theta,\mathbf X\rangle\\
\text{s.t.}\ &\mathbf X\succeq0,\quad X_{14,14}=1,\quad \langle\operatorname{sym}(\hat{\mathbf e}_{\rm tr}\mathbf e_{14}^\top),\mathbf X\rangle=1,\quad \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf X_{[10]})=1,\\
&\langle\operatorname{sym}(\hat{\mathbf c}_j\mathbf e_{14}^\top),\mathbf X\rangle\le0,\quad j=1,\dots,p,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shor}
\end{equation}
together with the linearised Veronese relations $2X_{ij}=X_{kk}$ and $S_{ik}S_{jl}=S_{il}S_{jk}$ in $\mathbf X$. Then $\mathrm{Shor}\le J^\star$. If the optimiser has rank one, its factor is a globally optimal feasible pose; if $\mathrm{Shor}\ge J(\hat{\mathbf z})-\tau_{\rm sdp}$ and $\hat{\mathbf z}$ is feasible, then $\hat{\mathbf z}$ is $\tau_{\rm sdp}$-globally optimal.
\end{lemma}
Every feasible $\mathbf z$ yields a feasible rank-one $\mathbf X$ with the same objective. The Veronese relations hold on $\nu_2(\mathbb{RP}^3)$ and are linear in $\mathbf X$; without them the relaxation is unbounded below and vacuous. On a synthetic $p=12$ instance the plain Shor value is vacuous ($-1\times10^{-10}$); with Veronese it is $0.0030334692$, matching the unconstrained QPEP value $0.0030334724$; adding the $p$ inequalities gives $\mathbf{0.0680069355}$, matching the ALM output $0.06800693548$ with $\lambda_2/\lambda_1=2.2\times10^{-12}$ (tight); the Shor relaxation of $d(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu})$ gives $0.0034784073$, a $0.0645$ gap three orders worse. We therefore make \eqref{eq:shor} the certificate of record and demote $d(\boldsymbol\mu)$ to a diagnostic.

\textbf{A rigorous dual repair (Theorem P2-Cert).} SCS \cite{odonoghue2016scs} is not dual-feasible and can return $\mathrm{Shor}>J$. Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} removes that tolerance. With $\mathbf y_{14}=\mathbf F\mathbf y_{13}$, $\mathbf y_{14}=[\mathbf v_2;\mathbf t;1]$, $\mathbf y_{13}=[\mathbf v_2;\mathbf t]$ and $\mathbf F$ the map that appends $\mathbf e_{\rm tr}^\top\mathbf v_2=1$, project the approximate dual to $\tilde{\boldsymbol\mu}=[\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}]_+\ge0$, $\tilde{\boldsymbol\Gamma}=[\hat{\boldsymbol\Gamma}]_+\ge0$, and assemble
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf H}_{13}=\mathbf F^\top\Big(\mathbf Q_{\rm aug}+\sum_j\tilde\mu_j\mathbf A_{g_j}-\sum_{i\le j}\tilde\Gamma_{ij}\mathbf A_{g_ig_j}-\hat\lambda_0\mathbf A_0-\sum_k\hat\lambda_k\mathbf B_k\Big)\mathbf F .
\label{eq:p2cert}
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[P2-Cert: rigorous dual repair]\label{thm:cert}
If $\tilde{\mathbf H}_{tt}\succ0$, let $\mathbf S_{10}=\tilde{\mathbf H}_{RR}-\tilde{\mathbf H}_{Rt}\tilde{\mathbf H}_{tt}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf H}_{Rt}^\top$ and $\delta_{\min}=\min(0,\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf S_{10}))$; otherwise let $\delta_{13}=\min(0,\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{\mathbf H}_{13}))$ and use $\|\mathbf t\|\le R_t$. Then
\begin{equation}
d_{\rm sound}=
\begin{cases}
\hat\lambda_0+\delta_{\min}-\delta_{\rm round}, & \tilde{\mathbf H}_{tt}\succ0,\\[1pt]
\hat\lambda_0+(1+R_t^2)(\delta_{13}-\delta_{\rm round}), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad d_{\rm sound}\le f^\star_{\rm global},
\label{eq:dsound}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\rm round}\sim10^{-12}\|\mathbf S_{10}\|_F$ is a floating-point margin certified by a Rump/Cholesky check.
\end{theorem}
Only $\tilde\mu,\tilde\Gamma\ge0$, $g_j\le0$, $g_ig_j\ge0$, the Veronese equalities and $\|\mathbf v_2(\mathbf q)\|^2=1$ enter, never the SDP accuracy, so $J-d_{\rm sound}\ge0$ \emph{by construction}; our check records one raw difference of $-6.7\times10^{-13}$, inside the margin. This is the bound of record, whereas $J-\mathrm{Shor}\le10^{-6}$ is \emph{not} a bound and serves only as an upper bound on the sound rate. The same proof diagnoses the six LM-O plane-only failures: a common normal leaves only $\mathbf n^\top\mathbf t$ constrained, and depth/tilt rows add the cross-direction products. The PSD block is $14\times14$ for every $p$, so the cost is $O(p\cdot14^3)$ per iteration and the certificate is solved asynchronously.

\subsection{Theorem 2.3 and Proposition 2.1: FB-reduced implicit differentiation}\label{sec:fb}
At a solution, stationarity and complementarity are $\nabla_{\mathbf z}\mathcal L=\mathbf 0\in\mathbb{R}^6$ and $0\le\mu_j^\star\perp(-g_j^\star)\ge0$. Differentiating them needs $d\mu_j$: strongly active constraints keep $dg_j=0$, inactive ones $d\mu_j=0$, and weakly active ones ($\mu_j=g_j=0$) satisfy neither. Classical active-set differentiation assumes full rank and strict complementarity; four contacts and a grazing contact violate both.

The FB smoothing replaces complementarity by $\varphi_\varepsilon(\mu_j,g_j)=\mu_j-g_j-\sqrt{\mu_j^2+g_j^2+\varepsilon^2}=0$. For $\varepsilon>0$ it is \emph{not} equivalent: its zero set is $\mu_jg_j=-\varepsilon^2/2$, and at a complementarity point it evaluates to $-\varepsilon^2/(2\mu_j)$ (strongly active), $-\varepsilon$ (weakly active) and $-\varepsilon^2/(2|g_j|)$ (inactive). It is a smooth surrogate for the sensitivity system only. With $r=\sqrt{\mu^2+g^2+\varepsilon^2}$, $\partial_\mu\varphi_\varepsilon=1-\mu/r=:a\in(0,1]$ and $\partial_g\varphi_\varepsilon=-(1+g/r)=:-b$, where $b\in[0,1]$ for $g\le0$ and $b\in(1,2)$ for $g>0$; $a\to\varepsilon^2/(2\mu^2)$ at a strongly active constraint, $b\to\varepsilon^2/(2g^2)$ at a strictly inactive one, and $a=b=1$ at a weakly active one. For the scalar kink $\min_z\tfrac12(z-a)^2$ s.t.\ $z\ge0$, the FB solution is $z_\varepsilon=\tfrac12(a+\sqrt{a^2+2\varepsilon^2})$, so $dz_\varepsilon/da|_{a=0}=1/2$ for every $\varepsilon$: it selects the midpoint of the Clarke derivative $[0,1]$. Whether this survives multiple weak-active constraints is open (G2).

Let $\mathcal A=\{j:a_j<\delta_{\rm act}\}$ and $\mathcal R$ its complement. Eliminating $d\mu_j=(b_j/a_j)(\mathbf J_{g,j}d\mathbf z+\partial_\theta g_j\,d\theta)$ for $j\in\mathcal R$, with $\mathbf H_{\rm eff}=\nabla^2_{\mathbf z}\mathcal L+\sum_{j\in\mathcal R}(b_j/a_j)\mathbf J_{g,j}^\top\mathbf J_{g,j}$,
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf H_{\rm eff}&\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top\\ \mathbf J_{\mathcal A}&-\mathbf D_a\end{bmatrix}}_{=:\mathbf S}\begin{bmatrix}d\mathbf z\\ d\boldsymbol\mu_{\mathcal A}\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}-\partial_\theta\nabla_{\mathbf z}\mathcal L-\sum_{j\in\mathcal R}\frac{b_j}{a_j}\mathbf J_{g,j}^\top\partial_\theta g_j\\[1pt] -\partial_\theta\mathbf g_{\mathcal A}\end{bmatrix},\quad
\mathbf D_a=\operatorname{diag}_{j\in\mathcal A}\Big(\frac{a_j}{b_j}\Big).
\label{eq:saddle}
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Invertibility under rank deficiency]\label{thm:fb}
If $\mathbf H_{\rm eff}\succ0$ and $\mathbf D_a\succ0$, then $\mathbf S$ is invertible for \emph{any} $\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}$, including rank-deficient ones; its Schur complement is $-(\mathbf D_a+\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}\mathbf H_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top)\prec0$. As $\varepsilon\to0$, $\mathbf S$ tends to $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf H_{\rm eff}&\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top\\ \mathbf J_{\mathcal A}&0\end{bmatrix}$, invertible iff $\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}$ has full row rank and $\mathbf H_{\rm eff}\succ0$ on $\ker\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}$ (LICQ and SOSC). The condition number scales as $\kappa(\mathbf S)\asymp1/\varepsilon^2$ when $\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}$ is rank deficient, and is bounded otherwise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proposition}[The $\varepsilon\to0$ limit]\label{prop:limit}
With $\mathbf r=-\partial_\theta\nabla_{\mathbf z}\mathcal L$ and $\mathbf w=-\partial_\theta\mathbf g_{\mathcal A}$, \eqref{eq:saddle} has a unique solution for every $\varepsilon>0$, and as $\varepsilon\to0$
\begin{equation}
d\mathbf z\to\mathbf H_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top\big(\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}\mathbf H_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top\big)^{+}\mathbf w+\big[\mathbf I-\mathbf P_{\mathcal A}\big]\mathbf H_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf r,
\label{eq:limit}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf P_{\mathcal A}=\mathbf H_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}^\top(\cdot)^{+}\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}$, the $\mathbf H_{\rm eff}$-weighted minimum-norm solution of $\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}d\mathbf z=\mathbf w$. If $\mathbf w\in\operatorname{range}(\mathbf J_{\mathcal A})$ (compatibility), $d\mathbf z^\star$ is the true derivative and is independent of the multiplier distribution; otherwise the multipliers diverge as $\|d\boldsymbol\mu_{\mathcal A}\|\asymp1/\varepsilon^2$ and $d\mathbf z^\star$ is a \emph{selected} generalized derivative of a non-differentiable map.
\end{proposition}
For the four-legged table, $\mathbf J_{\mathcal A}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times3}$ has rank 3. If $\theta$ moves the table rigidly, compatibility holds; if the four leg heights are independent it fails and the limit is one selected generalized derivative. A check gives error $1.3\times10^{-11}$ with bounded multipliers (difference $2.3\times10^{-9}$ over three distributions) in the compatible case, and $\|d\boldsymbol\mu\|=10^{-1}\to10^{6}$ as $\mathbf D_a$ goes $10^{-1}\to10^{-8}$ with $\|d\mathbf z\|$ staying at $0.707$ in the incompatible one. We adopt the Gauss--Newton choice $\mathbf H_{\rm tan}:=\nabla^2_{\mathbf z}J_\theta\succeq0$, dropping $\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}\mu_j\nabla^2_{\mathbf z}g_j$; then $\mathbf H_{\rm eff}\succ0$ whenever $\nabla^2J_\theta\succ0$, so the two results apply without an indefinite-Hessian limitation, at the price of differentiating the GN surrogate map (discrepancy $O(\sum_j\mu_j\|\nabla^2g_j\|)$, left to a future work ablation). The exact-Hessian variant is the exact KKT system but needs Levenberg damping in practice.

\subsection{The inner root structure: the $4/12/16/32/40$ spectrum}\label{sec:spectrum}
Let $N_{\mathbb C}$ be the number of isolated complex projective roots of $\nabla f(\mathbf q)=\lambda\mathbf q$, $\mathbf q^\top\mathbf q=1$, modulo $\mathbf q\leftrightarrow-\mathbf q$.
\begin{theorem}[Inner root spectrum]\label{thm:spectrum}
(i) If $\mathbf M^{\rm red}_{\mathcal A}$ is PnP-type ($\mathbf M^{\rm red}_{\mathcal A}\mathbf e_{\rm tr}=\mathbf 0$; rank 9 at our noise level, rank 8 noise-free), then $N_{\mathbb C}=40$ with the generic 15-monomial staircase: $\mathbf M\mapsto\mathbf M+\alpha\mathbf I_{10}$ changes $f$ by $\alpha(\mathbf q^\top\mathbf q)^2$, so every critical point survives and a homotopy finding only 16--18 real roots is behaving as expected. (ii) For an isotropic 3D--3D (Horn) base with $k$ plane constraints sharing one normal, the Hopf ruled line $\mathcal L_{\mathbf n}\cong\mathbb{CP}^1$ is an excess component; saturating it gives $N_{\mathbb C}=4$ ($k=0$), $12$ ($k=1$), $16$ ($k\ge2$), $32$ for two distinct normals, and $40$ for an anisotropic/PnP base with any plane.
\end{theorem}
A homotopy study on 24 PnP instances finds all 40 finite projective roots (0 lost), with 16 real roots on 20 instances and 18 on 4; the random-$\mathbf M$ control gives 40/40/40/40/40. On the isotropic family the enumerator reads $4/12/16/16/16$ projective ($8/24/32/32/32$ affine) after re-polishing divergent paths, while its raw census reads 12 at $k=2$ because four genuine roots at $\|\mathbf q\|\approx29$--$34$ cross the divergence tolerance. Only the 40-root \emph{unsaturated} template is shipped and exercised end-to-end; the spectrum is validated numerically in canonical form, but the $16\times16$ saturated action matrix is \emph{not} a shipped template.

\subsection{The open questions that bound our claims}\label{sec:open}
\textbf{G1} (resolved by Thm.~\ref{thm:spectrum}): the generic count stays 40 for Type-I bases. \textbf{G2} (open): the generalized derivative selected by FB at weak-active points is characterised only for the scalar kink, so we say ``a selected generalized derivative'', never ``the derivative''. \textbf{G3} (resolved by Thm.~\ref{thm:cert}): soundness no longer depends on the SDP tolerance; tightness remains empirical (G4). \textbf{G5}: the quantitative GN bias is a future work ablation.
