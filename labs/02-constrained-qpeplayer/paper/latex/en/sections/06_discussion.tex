\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What the layer buys.} Hard constraints at a cost independent of their number, a derivative that survives rank-deficient and weakly active contacts, and posteriori evidence of globality at cost independent of $p$---physical admissibility, trainability and a failure signal. \textbf{What it does not buy} is globality of the forward solve: the ALM is a KKT method and the certificate can fail. We consider this the right trade, since a layer that says ``uncertified'' is more useful than one that says ``certified'' incorrectly.

\textbf{The four-leg example is narrower than it looks.} The flagship LICQ-failure case is differentiable (and our method is exact) when the parameter moves the support surface rigidly, and non-differentiable when the contact heights are independent. In our real-data protocol the support plane is estimated once per frame from depth and is therefore a \emph{rigid} input parameter, so compatibility holds; the non-rigid case arises when the four contact heights are learned, which we do not do.

\textbf{Bias and variance of the certificate.} The relaxation is tight on our synthetic instance and on the unconstrained sub-case, but tightness is empirical, not a theorem. On real data it is tight at a dataset-dependent rate: 24.4/15.3/13.1\% under base cuts, 51.1/41.7/23.8\% with the sound bound-RLT family (Table~\ref{tab:rigorous}). Where it fails, the claim reduces to ``sound lower bound plus a feasibility check'', which still bounds suboptimality but is not certification; the uncertified rows are genuinely slack (median gap $1$--$2\times10^{-5}$), not numerically inconclusive. Soundness is no longer empirical: with the dual repair of Thm.~\ref{thm:cert}, every reported rigorous gap is non-negative by construction, so the failure mode is \emph{slack}, never a false certificate.

\textbf{Scope.} Non-affine constraints (distance, clearance) would break the constant-dimension reduction because $g_j^2$ would no longer be a quadratic form in $(\mathbf v_2,\mathbf t)$; branch-crossing cases need a mixture/Laplace model, a different paper in this programme. The method is deliberately orthogonal to the other two prototypes: it assumes a single constrained optimum and a unimodal posterior, and spends its budget on constraints and rank-robust differentiation.
