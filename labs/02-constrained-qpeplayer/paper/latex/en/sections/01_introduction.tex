\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A 6-DoF pose layer maps learned quantities $\theta$ to a pose $(\mathbf R,\mathbf t)$ differentiably. \emph{Local} layers---BPnP \cite{chen2020bpn}, Theseus \cite{pineda2022theseus}, and the Gauss--Newton (GN) pipeline in most SLAM systems---solve a nonlinear least-squares problem from the current estimate and differentiate the KKT system of that \emph{local} solution. \emph{Global} layers---SDPRLayers \cite{holmes2025sdpr}---solve a convex semidefinite relaxation and differentiate the original non-convex KKT system using the relaxation's dual certificate. Both were designed for \emph{equality} constraints: SDPRLayers states its class as a homogeneous quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP) with $\mathbf x^\top\mathbf A_i\mathbf x=0$ \cite{holmes2025sdpr}, and QPEP \cite{wu2022qpep} has exactly one, $\|\mathbf q\|^2=1$.

Real pose problems are \emph{inequality} constrained: an object on a table cannot penetrate it, a point in front of the camera has positive depth, and a camera's field of view bounds where a point projects. These are physical admissibility conditions, not soft preferences, and a layer that violates them returns poses a motion planner must reject.

Adding $p$ slacks to an SDP layer lifts the PSD matrix from $10\times10$ to $(10+p)\times(10+p)$---for a 50-vertex mesh a $60\times60$ SDP \emph{per pose per training step} on the CPU, with interior-point cost $O((10+p)^6)$---and the backward pass then needs tightness and identifiable multipliers, whereas inequality-constrained relaxations routinely violate strict complementarity ($g_j^\star=\mu_j^\star=0$). Generic differentiable QP layers---dQP \cite{magoon2025dqp}, OptNet \cite{amos2017optnet}, CVXPYLayers \cite{agrawal2019cvxpylayers}---handle linear inequalities natively, but the rotation manifold is not convex and their active-set machinery inherits the LICQ assumption that fails at multi-contact configurations. Soft penalties \cite{pineda2022theseus} bound penetration by a scene-dependent weight rather than by zero; DC3 \cite{donti2021dc3} corrects constraints at inference time without differentiating the correction; QPEP \cite{wu2022qpep} is algebraically global given a complete enumeration, but has no gradient and no inequalities.

We ask whether a pose layer can have hard inequality constraints, a per-sample cost independent of the number of constraints, differentiability that survives redundant and weakly active contacts, and posteriori evidence of global optimality. \textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Additive constant-dimension reduction} (Thm.~\ref{thm:dim}). For constraints affine in $(\mathbf v_2(\mathbf q),\mathbf t)$---positive depth, planar non-penetration, field of view, tilt cones---each augmented-Lagrangian (ALM) inner subproblem, restricted to an active set, collapses after a $3\times3$ Schur complement to a quartic form on $\mathbb{S}^3$ independent of $p$: $O(p)$ assembly, $O(1)$ memory.
\item \emph{A sound posteriori certificate with a $p$-independent PSD block} (Lemma~\ref{lem:cert}). A $14\times14$ Veronese-augmented Shor relaxation gives a rigorous lower bound and certifies global optimality when tight, for every $p$; Thm.~\ref{thm:cert} (P2-Cert) repairs the solver dual so that every reported gap is non-negative by construction. On a controlled instance the relaxation is tight to $2\times10^{-11}$ while the Lagrangian-dual gap is negative ($-1.67\times10^{-11}$), which makes the relaxation, not the dual function, the bound of record.
\item \emph{FB-reduced implicit differentiation with a precise limit theory} (Thm.~\ref{thm:fb}, Prop.~\ref{prop:limit}). A $(6+|\mathcal A|)\times(6+|\mathcal A|)$ saddle-point system is invertible for every $\varepsilon>0$ with \emph{no rank assumption} on the active Jacobian; its limit is the $H$-weighted minimum-norm solution, which is the true derivative under a checkable compatibility condition and \emph{a selected} generalized derivative otherwise.
\item \emph{A certifiable layer interface.} The layer returns pose, certificate value, feasibility violation, active set and conditioning, so downstream code can branch on certification; we evaluate on synthetic desktop instances and on the three BOP datasets at layer level.
\end{enumerate}
All constraints considered in this paper are affine in $(\mathbf v_2(\mathbf q),\mathbf t)$; non-affine constraints such as distance and clearance bounds lie outside this class.
