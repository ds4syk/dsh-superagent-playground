\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \textsc{Laplace-QPEPLayer}, a differentiable pose layer that combines the quaternion algebra of QPEP with a $3\times3$ Riemannian implicit differentiation and a multi-modal Laplace posterior. The theoretical core is an exact orthogonal bridge between the rotation lift and the Veronese embedding, a proof that the 21 standard $SO(3)$ constraints span the degree-two Veronese ideal, a certified Choi--Lam gap instance of $0.034188$ with $\operatorname{rank}(X^\star)=6$, a three-part theorem relating the $10\times10$ relaxation to the released $13\times13$ one, and a closed-form backward pass with antipodal invariance. The probabilistic core is an exact translation integral and an analytic Laplace sum over all enumerated rotation minima, with explicit error order and failure modes. The design uses no SDP lift at inference, no Monte Carlo sampling, no inequality constraints and no outlier kernel.

On the synthetic 6D crossing the Laplace map is smooth while the hard argmin is a step map, and the Laplace gradient agrees with a Richardson FD to $1.8\times10^{-7}$; the 1D exact-posterior checks give an $O(\tau)$ error with log-log slopes 1.066 and 1.036; the rotation SDP is certified at a gap of $0.034188$; the released $24$-constraint relaxation is looser by $1.8207$ on a physical six-point instance ($5.415$ versus $3.59431373$); and a total-degree homotopy confirms 40 complex projective roots on $4/4$ instances.

On 15 real LINEMOD frames with a genuine two-mode cost, the hard argmin is accurate at the near-ties, at the level of a classical EPnP/SQPNP (median $2.06^\circ$, $3.7$ mm ADD-S), while the Laplace mean is pulled toward the flip ($44.24^\circ$, $13.9$ mm, bias $+36.9^\circ$). The layer buys a deterministic, sampling-free gradient that is smooth at branch crossings, and it pays for that gradient in pose accuracy precisely on the frames where two modes are near-tied.
