\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

The layer provides hard constraints at a per-sample cost independent of their number, a derivative that survives rank-deficient and weakly active contacts, and posteriori evidence of globality at a cost independent of $p$. Its forward solve remains a KKT method, so the certificate can be slack; on such samples the layer returns a feasible KKT point together with a sound lower bound on suboptimality, which suits a pipeline that must reject inadmissible poses. Tightness is empirical rather than proved: it holds by three orders of magnitude on the synthetic instances and at dataset-dependent rates of 24.4/15.3/13.1\% and 51.1/41.7/23.8\% on real data (Table~\ref{tab:rigorous}), the uncertified rows failing by a median gap of $10^{-5}$, i.e.\ by relaxation slack rather than solver error. The study is layer-level and uses ground-truth-projected correspondences, so the accuracy figures characterise the constrained solve rather than a perception system. End-to-end training with learned correspondences, non-affine constraints such as clearance, and parameter regions where optima exchange are the natural next steps.
