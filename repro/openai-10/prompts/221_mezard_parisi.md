# Problem: the Mézard–Parisi formula for diluted spin glasses

Fix an even integer \(p\ge2\), a real number \(\alpha>0\), a random function
\(\theta:\{-1,1\}^p\to\mathbb R\), and a real random variable \(h\). Assume
\[
\mathbb E\max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^p}|\theta(s)|+\mathbb E|h|<\infty.
\]
Require the interaction hypotheses of Panchenko--Talagrand (1.2)--(1.4): for all
\(s=(s_1,\ldots,s_p)\),
\[
e^{\theta(s)}=a\left(1+b\prod_{\ell=1}^p f_\ell(s_\ell)\right),\qquad
\left|b\prod_{\ell=1}^p f_\ell(s_\ell)\right|<1\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
where the \(f_\ell:\{-1,1\}\to\mathbb R\) are independent copies of one random function, \(b\) is
independent of them, and \(\mathbb E[(-b)^n]\ge0\) for every integer \(n\ge1\). The positive random
variable \(a\) may depend on the other factorization variables.

For each \(N\), let \(M_N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\alpha N)\), let \((i_{\ell,k})\) be
independent uniform indices in \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\), let \((\theta_k)\) be independent copies of
\(\theta\), and let \((h_i)\) be independent copies of \(h\). These families are mutually
independent; indices are sampled with replacement. For \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^N\), set
\[
-H_N(\sigma)=\sum_{k=1}^{M_N}\theta_k(\sigma_{i_{1,k}},\ldots,\sigma_{i_{p,k}})
+\sum_{i=1}^N h_i\sigma_i,\qquad
F_N=\frac1N\mathbb E\log\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^N}e^{-H_N(\sigma)}.
\]
All model parameters and disorder laws are fixed as \(N\to\infty\); any inverse-temperature
parameter is absorbed into \(\theta\) and \(h\).

Define \(q_x(s)=e^{xs}/(2\cosh x)\) for \(s\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathbb R\), and the cavity
quantities
\[
C_\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_p)=\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^p}e^{\theta(s)}
\prod_{\ell=1}^p q_{x_\ell}(s_\ell),
\]
\[
U_\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_{p-1};\varepsilon)
=\log\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^{p-1}}e^{\theta(s_1,\ldots,s_{p-1},\varepsilon)}
\prod_{\ell=1}^{p-1}q_{x_\ell}(s_\ell).
\]

Fix an integer \(r\ge1\), a law \(\zeta\in\mathcal M_{r+1}\), and
\(0<m_1<\cdots<m_r<1\), where \(\mathcal M_1=\mathcal P(\mathbb R)\) and
\(\mathcal M_{j+1}=\mathcal P(\mathcal M_j)\), with \(\mathcal P\) denoting Borel probability
measures and each space carrying its usual weak topology. For each message label \(\omega\),
independently generate a chain
\[
\eta_0^\omega\sim\zeta,\qquad
\eta_{j+1}^\omega\mid\eta_j^\omega\sim\eta_j^\omega\quad(0\le j\le r-2),\qquad
x^\omega\mid\eta_{r-1}^\omega\sim\eta_{r-1}^\omega.
\]
Thus \(\eta_j^\omega\in\mathcal M_{r-j}\). Generate all chains independently of the physical
disorder. Use distinct labels for \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\) and for \(x_\ell^j\), \(j\ge1\),
\(1\le\ell\le p-1\). Take mutually independent \(k\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\alpha p)\),
\(\theta,\theta_1,\theta_2,\ldots\) with the prescribed interaction law, and \(h\) with the
prescribed field law. Put
\[
V_{\rm s}=\frac12\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm1}\exp\left(h\varepsilon+\sum_{j=1}^k
U_{\theta_j}(x_1^j,\ldots,x_{p-1}^j;\varepsilon)\right),\qquad
V_{\rm e}=C_\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_p).
\]
For \(0\le\ell<r\), let \(\mathcal F_\ell\) be generated by all physical disorder just listed and by
\(\eta_j^\omega\) for all labels \(\omega\) and \(0\le j\le\ell\). For either positive variable
\(V\) above, define backwards
\[
T_rV=V,\qquad
T_\ell V=\left(\mathbb E\left[(T_{\ell+1}V)^{m_{\ell+1}}\mid\mathcal F_\ell\right]\right)^{1/m_{\ell+1}}
\quad(\ell=r-1,\ldots,0).
\]
Thus these conditional expectations average auxiliary messages only; the outer expectation below
also averages the physical disorder and the root measures. Set
\[
\mathcal B_r(\zeta,m)=\log2+\mathbb E\log T_0V_{\rm s}
-\alpha(p-1)\mathbb E\log T_0V_{\rm e},
\]
\[
\Phi_r=\inf_{\substack{\zeta\in\mathcal M_{r+1}\\0<m_1<\cdots<m_r<1}}\mathcal B_r(\zeta,m).
\]
At level \(r=0\), instead take all messages independently with a common arbitrary law
\(\zeta\in\mathcal M_1\), omit the operators \(T_\ell\), and define
\[
\Phi_0=\inf_{\zeta\in\mathcal M_1}\left\{\log2+\mathbb E\log V_{\rm s}
-\alpha(p-1)\mathbb E\log V_{\rm e}\right\}.
\]

**Task.** Prove or disprove that, for every model satisfying these hypotheses, the thermodynamic
limit exists and
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_N=\inf_{r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}\Phi_r.
\]
The infimum runs over all finite replica-symmetry-breaking levels, all their hierarchical message
laws, and all admissible breakpoints. No attainment at a fixed level or by a particular trial law is
asserted.
