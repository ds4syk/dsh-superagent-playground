\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{No global guarantee.} The inner weighted QPEP is globally solved, but the outer
GNC is not. A GNC fixed point is a stationary point of $F_{\mathrm{rob}}$; it need not be a local
minimum (if $H_{\mathrm{rob}}$ is indefinite) and need not be the global minimum. The
$\mu$-homotopy can bifurcate, and the algorithm can follow a branch that is not globally optimal.
We state this explicitly and do not use the phrase ``global robust layer''.

\noindent\textbf{Model breakdown.} Even a perfect global solver for $F_{\mathrm{rob}}$ can return
the wrong pose when coherent outliers outnumber inliers. A one-dimensional example with one inlier
at $0$ and two coherent outliers at $M$ has $F(0)\to2c^2$ and $F(M)\to c^2$ as $M\to\infty$, so the
global minimum is the outlier pose. This is an identifiability limit, not an algorithmic bug;
practical deployment needs a consensus check, an inlier-count diagnostic, or a prior on the
outlier fraction.

\noindent\textbf{Boundary outliers are the worst.} $\lambda_\parallel$ is most negative at $e=c^2$,
not at $e\to\infty$. Gross outliers are gradient-immune at $O(\|\mathbf{r}\|^{-4})$, but a point
just beyond the redescending threshold has the largest gradient influence---a warning for training,
and an opportunity, because boundary outliers are where the curvature correction matters most.

\noindent\textbf{Gradient immunity versus robustness.} The per-observation bound is conditional on
a uniform positive lower bound on $H_{\mathrm{rob}}$. If the Hessian is nearly singular the constant
blows up; if many outliers align their contributions add; if the outliers are coherent the global
minimum can be wrong. We therefore separate ``the gradient through this observation decays'' from
``the estimator is robust''. The framework covers GM, Cauchy, Welsch, and TLS, but the scale $c$ is
critical: a fixed $c$ may fail under varying noise, a learned $c$ is scale-identifiable only with a
normalization such as $\det W_i=1$, and sensitivity to $c$ should be reported. The derivations are
exact for residuals affine in $\mathbf{t}$ and quadratic in the lifted quaternion; reprojection
error is not polynomial, QPEP uses an algebraic surrogate, and extending $\lambda_i$ to it is future
work.

\noindent\textbf{Fairness against RANSAC.} RANSAC is a strong, well-tuned baseline. Our claim is
not ``RANSAC is bad''; it is ``a differentiable robust layer can remove the non-differentiable
wrapper and provide useful gradients, at the cost of a local robust estimator''. Against
fixed-budget RANSAC the speedup is $22$--$32\times$; against adaptive-budget RANSAC it is slower at
low outlier fractions, at parity near $40\%$, and about $8\times$ faster at $70\%$; LO-RANSAC ties
accuracy but is itself $1.03$--$1.52\times$ slower than adaptive RANSAC. Any ``faster than RANSAC''
sentence must name the budget.

\noindent\textbf{Real-data coverage and what the fallbacks do not prove.} The real evidence is all
eight official 3DMatch test scenes, two KITTI raw drives, all 11 KITTI odometry sequences 00--10 (33
selected frames each), and two daylight plus two night-prefix In-The-Dark runs. The 54-scene
3DMatch RGB-D training corpus is not used, and the true $0.20$--$0.50$ geometric-overlap band still
has oracle base inlier $0.88$--$0.99$ because every GT-NN match passes the $\tau$ gate, so lower
real-descriptor overlap is not covered. In-The-Dark is no longer download-blocked---the official GT
is composed from repeat spatial $\times$ teach temporal $\times$ camera-frame conjugation, and
verification passes on both runs---but the night windows are 10 pairs each and the front-end is
hand-written, so this is not SDPR-level or full-night evidence; the night inlier advantage is
motion/depth-confounded, as the daylight control shows. The KITTI stereo fallback is
rotation-positive and translation-negative ($0\%$ at $0.1$~m) and is not a substitute. The
GPU/CUDA-graph probe is an engineering evaluation of the $4\times4$ inner only, and its GPU leg is
blocked by Slurm GPU occupancy (job 3184 was PENDING with a 21~h estimate and was cancelled; only
CPU validation ran).

\noindent\textbf{When to use the layer.} Use it when (i) matches are noisy and contain gross
outliers, (ii) end-to-end gradients through the pose are needed, (iii) the outlier fraction is below
the breakdown regime, and (iv) a $6\times6$ backward and a fixed-size algebraic forward fit the
compute budget. Do not use it when certified global optimality or adversarial robustness is
required.
